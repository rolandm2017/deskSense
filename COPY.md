# COPY GUIDANCE

DeskSense copy should feel like a mirror, not a manager.
It reflects reality with clarity, and occasionally hints at possibility—but never judges, commands, or dramatizes.

## Tone Principles

Observational, not evaluative
(“You’ve logged 40m” > “You’re behind”)
Calmly optimistic when possible
(“There’s still time” > “Hurry up”)
Neutral when final
(“That’s today” > “You missed your goal”)
Short, varied phrasing
Avoid repetitive “You are…” structures
Emotionally lightweight
No guilt, no hype, no coaching energy

## States & Example Copy

1. Start of Day (Open / Not Started)

Intent: Spacious, neutral beginning. No pressure.

The day’s just getting started.
Nothing on the board yet.
A fresh start.
Plenty of time ahead.
Ready when you are.
The day is open. 2. On Pace (Early / Steady Progress)

Intent: Light affirmation. No exaggeration.

A solid start.
You’re on track.
Progress is building.
Good pace so far.
Moving in the right direction.
That’s a good rhythm. 3. Slightly Ahead

Intent: Subtle positive reinforcement, not celebration.

You’re a bit ahead.
Off to a strong start.
Ahead of pace.
More than enough for now.
You’ve built a buffer. 4. Far Ahead

Intent: Moderate high-five, still composed.

Well ahead of pace.
You’ve got this covered.
More than enough today.
You’re comfortably past the mark.
Plenty done already. 5. Slipping (Behind, Recoverable)

Intent: Acknowledge reality + preserve agency.

Some progress so far. There’s still time.
You’ve made a start—room to keep going.
The day’s open if you want to add more.
A bit light so far.
Still within reach.

(Avoid: “behind”, “falling short”, “you need to…”)

6. Close (Near Goal)

Intent: Gentle anticipation.

You’re getting close.
Nearly there.
Within reach.
Just about there.
Closing in. 7. Hit (Goal Met)

Intent: Warm acknowledgment, not celebration overload.

That’s the target.
Goal met.
You hit it.
Nicely done.
That’s a full day. 8. Slightly Over (Just Past Goal)

Intent: Quiet satisfaction.

Just past the mark.
A little extra today.
You’ve gone beyond.
More than planned. 9. Far Ahead (Well Beyond Goal)

Intent: Strong but still grounded.

Well past the goal.
A full and then some.
You went well beyond today.
Plenty of time logged. 10. Missed (End of Day, Below Goal)

Intent: Neutral, archival. No emotional steering.

That’s where the day landed.
That’s today.
Short of the target, but logged.
That’s what made it in.
The day wraps here.

## Structural Patterns

Use variation across these patterns to avoid monotony:

Observational
“40m logged so far.”
“1h 30m on the board.”
State-based
“On track.”
“Within reach.”
“Ahead of pace.”
Temporal framing
“Still time.”
“The day is open.”
“That’s today.”
Soft encouragement (optional)
“If you want to add more.”
“Room to keep going.”
What to Avoid
Language to exclude
“behind”
“should”
“need to”
“only”
“just X left” (can feel pressuring)
“missed your goal” (too evaluative)
Tone to avoid
Coaching (“Let’s get back on track”)
Authority (“You need to focus”)
Guilt (“You wasted time”)
Hype (“Amazing!!!”)
Heuristics for Writing New Copy

When adding new lines, check:

Would this feel annoying after 100 days?
Does this imply judgment, even subtly?
Does this preserve user agency?
Is it shorter than it could be?
Does it sound like a calm observation, not advice?

If unsure, default to:

“That’s where things stand.”

## Why this works (design rationale)

You’re implicitly creating 5 emotional states:

Open (start of day)
In motion (on track)
Recoverable (slipping)
Imminent success (close)
Complete / Final (hit or missed)

## Tone rules:

State | Tone
Start | Spacious
On track | Affirming
Slipping | Calm + possibility
Close | Light excitement
Hit | Warm acknowledgment
Missed | Neutral acceptance

## High variance in copy required

Make this feel even more crafted by avoiding repeated structure.

Instead of always:

“You are X”

You vary the sentence shape:

The day’s just getting started.
A solid start.
Some progress so far.
You’re getting close.
That’s the target.
That’s where the day landed.

This gives it that quiet, intentional product voice instead of “system messages.”

## Minimalism encouraged

Lean minimal:

7am → Just getting started.
11am → Good pace.
3pm → Still time.
6pm → Close.
9pm → Hit.
11pm → That’s today.

## Concluding antipatterns to avoid

Avoid ever saying:

“behind”
“should”
“need to”
“only X done”

Those flip the app from mirror → authority

The whole product thesis (from PRODUCT.md) is:

"accurately satisfied, or dissatisfied — decided by the user"
